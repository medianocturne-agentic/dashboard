How the Agentic system works

A plain-English guide to what this account does, why, and the safety net that protects it. No jargon left unexplained — there's a glossary at the bottom. The strategy was rebuilt on July 29, 2026 into the "open-burst time-hold" described here.

Note: This is a live test built from a single morning's backtest — the edge is not yet proven. Judge it across many sessions, not any one day. The account risks only its own small stake and holds nothing overnight.

The one-line idea

At the 9:30am ET open we buy the day's fastest movers — the names already ripping hardest off the open — put the whole account into them, and hold for about 30 minutes on the clock. Then we sell everything and go back to cash. That's the entire system. We're flat well before lunch and hold nothing overnight. The universe is a live scan of the biggest gappers plus a daily-movers list plus a big set of leveraged/inverse ETFs, so there's always something moving to buy — direction doesn't matter, live momentum does.

The daily rhythm

8:30am CT open · Buy the 12 fastest movers, whole account → Next ~30 min · Hold; check every 2 min for a name rolling over → +30 min · Sell everything to cash → Next morning · Rebuild

Because it's a cash account, proceeds take a day to settle (T+1) — but since we finish the day's one round-trip inside 30 minutes and never rebuy those proceeds the same day, that's never a constraint. Nothing is ever held overnight — zero overnight or weekend gap risk, the single biggest way to get hurt in leveraged funds.

How we pick what to buy

We rank the whole board by speed off the open — each name's move from yesterday's close, minus its bid-ask spread, so a wide illiquid quote can't fake a fast mover. Highest score first. We buy the top 12 and spread the account across them, with extra weight tilted into the very fastest; if fewer than 12 clean names qualify, we concentrate the account into the ones that do. The only thing that disqualifies a name is a junk filter — it has to be liquid (real volume and dollar-volume, a tight enough spread, price over $1). Being up a lot or extended is not a reason to skip it — that's the whole signal. We deploy the entire stake fractionally, so only pocket change ever sits idle.

How we protect it — and how we take the win

There's no profit target and no scalping. We're in for a short, defined window to catch a big upside burst, so we let the winners run to the clock and cut only the names that turn against us — fast. Two things, and only two things, sell a position early or on time.

The dual stop CUT LOSERS FAST

Checked every ~2 minutes from the first check after entry — no "warm-up" delay. Each name is cut at whichever line is higher: a hard −6% floor from our entry (so a name that dumps off the open is out near −6%, never left to bleed), or an 8% trailing give-back from its own high (so a name that runs up and then rolls over 8% off its peak is sold, banking the move). Early on the −6% floor protects; once a name has climbed, the trailing line lifts above it and locks in gains.

The +30-minute clock TIME EXIT

Everything still standing 30 minutes after entry is sold at market, all at once. The hold is the strategy; the clock is the default exit. We don't sit and hope past the window.

Hard flatten & overnight net ZERO OVERNIGHT

Right after the 30-minute window (~9:08am CT) a dedicated run force-sells anything still open, so we're 100% cash before lunch. A second, silent safety run late in the afternoon catches anything a morning outage could have left behind. The account cannot carry a position overnight.

Why it changed — from "ride & ratchet" to "open-burst"

July 29, 2026 — The rebuild

The prior system bought the same kind of fast movers but tried to manage them all day — banking half at +4%, riding the rest on a trailing stop measured on 5-minute closes, flat by mid-afternoon. Across a week it bled: the scalp capped winners while the close-based stop and long hold gave back gains and, worse, let a few names that dumped off the open ride deep into the red before a stop looked.

The fix: shorten everything. A backtest of simply buying at the open and selling on a fixed clock came back strong, so we cut the whole apparatus down to buy the fastest movers, hold ~30 minutes, cut the ones that turn, sell the rest on the clock. No scalp, no ratchet, no indicator gates. Faster stop checks (every 2 minutes, no warm-up) close the hole that let early dumps run. It is a live test — watch the ledger across sessions.

Glossary

Fastest mover (move − spread)
How we rank the board: a name's percent move from yesterday's close, minus its bid-ask spread. Subtracting the spread stops a wide, illiquid quote from masquerading as a real fast mover.
Leveraged / inverse-leveraged ETF
A fund built to move 2–3× a stock or index each day; "inverse" ones move the opposite way, so we can profit from a fall as easily as a rise. Big multipliers up and down — which is why the safety net matters.
The whole nut
The account's entire tradeable balance for the day. We deploy all of it across the basket — fractionally — so only sub-$1 pocket change is ever left uninvested.
Tilt to the top
After spreading the account across the basket, any leftover cash is pushed into the highest-ranked names — the fastest movers get the biggest bets.
High-water mark
The highest price a name has reached since we bought it. The trailing part of our stop measures 8% down from this, and it only ever ratchets up.
Dual stop
Our only early exit: sell at whichever is higher of a −6% floor from entry or 8% below the high-water mark. The floor cuts early dumps; the trailing line banks a name that runs then turns.
Time exit (+30 min)
The default exit — everything still held 30 minutes after entry is sold at market, all together.
Flatten
Sell everything to 100% cash. We flatten on the clock and confirm it with a hard flatten right after the window.
T+1 settlement
Cash from a sale isn't spendable until the next trading day. We never rebuy the same day's proceeds, so it never constrains us.
